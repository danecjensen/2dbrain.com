One effective hack is to always leave a trace by like "hearting" a Twitter post or "upvoting" a HN post or "starring" a GitHub repo. Your brain will usually remember where you read something so you just have to go to that site and then search through your activity.

This simple system leverages **context-dependent memory** - your brain naturally associates content with the platform where you encountered it. By consistently marking interesting content, you create a searchable personal archive within each platform's activity history.

## Why This Works

- **Platform-specific recall**: You often remember "I saw this on Twitter" or "This was on Hacker News"
- **Low friction**: One click to save, built into your existing workflow
- **Native search**: Each platform provides tools to search your own activity
- **No extra tools**: Uses features that already exist

## Implementation Tips

- Be consistent with your marking system
- Use Twitter likes/bookmarks for threads and discussions
- Star GitHub repos for code references
- Upvote HN posts for articles and discussions
- Save Reddit posts for communities and guides

This complements more formal note-taking systems by capturing your browsing history without interrupting your flow.
